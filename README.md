## Purpose

There is more than just an understanding of electrical engineering principles that you need when pursuing a career in technical sales engineer. You need to know how to communicate technical information effectively, know your customer's needs, and show how engineering products can satisfy their needs. Your required coursework will build your technical foundation, however you need more professional development as well to help set you apart from other applicants. Having relevant experience throughout your final two years as an electrical engineering student can help further develop your technical and interpersonal skills which is crucial for sales engineers [^1]. This report will outline the best practices for developing the necessary knowledge, professional skills, and experience to pursue a career in technical sales engineering after graduating.

## Developing Technical Expertise

The best way to go about developing technical expertise is through projects as well as hands-on experimentation. For example, building circuits, testing different electrical components, or even using different simulation software can help build your ability to evaluate different types of engineering products. 

**Build a portfolio study around a design decision** You should use a personal project of circuit lab and record the requirement, any alternatives you considered, and the measurements that support your choice. For example, you could compare two filters using measured gain at the frequency you want to preserve and the frequency you want to suppress.

## Building Communication and Sales Skills

That same circuit could support different recommendations based on what a customer might need. Someone that needs to detect any small changes in resistance cares about sensitivity while someone that has to estimate an unknown resistance cares more about accuracy. Figure 1 shows how a customer's needs determine what you show when demonstrating a Wheatstone bridge.

**Practice technical presentations with a peer outside of engineering** You can start by asking which result matters to them, show the appropriate measurement that addresses it, explain what that result means for their decision, then ask if they have any further questions about the recommendation you give them. Experiences like this can help you build your confidence when you interact with future clients.

## Gaining Professional Experience

You should pursue internships and networking events such as career fairs that are related to engineering and technical sales. Positions where you have to apply engineering, product support, or technical sales are great ways to gain relevant experience. Participating in engineering clubs and attending career fairs are opportunities for you to connect with professionals in the industry. When looking into these opportunities, you should prioritize the experiences that involve both technical problem solving as well as customer engagement.

## Planning the Final Two Years

Consistent development throughout your final two years is necessary in order to prepare for a career in technical sales. As opposed to waiting for the last minute to prepare for graduation, you should spread different goals across each quarter. ## Preparing for Employment

You should prepare for employment by tailoring your resume, portfolio, and your interview responses to the standards of technical sales positions. Focus on engineering projects, internships, and experiences that involve customer engagement. If you talk about technical projects, explain what the issue was, your method, and what the outcome was. Practicing technical interview questions can help communicate your qualifications clearly. You can present yourself as someone who is capable of understanding both the product and people-side.
